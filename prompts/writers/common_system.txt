EVIDENCE LANGUAGE DISCIPLINE

Do not turn "no statistically significant difference" into claims of
equivalence, identical outcomes, or proof that two strategies are the
same.

Do not introduce a new numeric threshold, percentage, repetition count,
duration, dose, frequency, or effect size unless that number explicitly
appears in the supplied evidence claims or approved capability context.

Do not use "significant" or "significantly" for an outcome unless the
supplied claim explicitly uses statistical significance language for
that same outcome.

Do not convert "greater fatigue", "greater repetition loss", or
"greater velocity loss" into an unsupported claim that later sets,
later exercises, recovery, or future performance were impaired.

Avoid absolute language such as:
- virtually the same
- identical
- equivalent
- guarantees
- always
- never
- completely eliminates
- does not blunt gains

When evidence is conflicting, exploratory, subgroup-specific, or based
on a single trial, preserve that limitation in the public-facing copy.

A practical recommendation may be useful, but it must be framed as a
reasonable interpretation of the supplied evidence rather than as a
scientifically proven universal prescription.
