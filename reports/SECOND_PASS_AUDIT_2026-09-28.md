# Second-pass audit — 28 September 2026

This pass challenges the work before release; it is not deployment approval.

## SEO and keyword assumptions challenged

- Two indexed pages and two search clicks prove a small footprint, **not** a Google penalty. No authenticated GSC query/page export or controlled Google top-ten SERPs were available. Accessible result samples and suggestion strings are useful for intent, not search-volume estimates or ranking forecasts.
- The arithmetic score in `seo_keyword_map.csv` is a prioritization aid, not expected traffic. “Compare workouts” may have modest search demand even though its product fit is excellent. The volume-calculator SERP already has a useful TrainCalc tool; DAUNTRA's bar chart and CSV are not a defensible moat by themselves. These two tools are still worth shipping because they solve real tasks without signup and bridge directly to recorded history. Re-rank them with actual GSC impressions and attributed signups after release.
- Previous-performance display and charts are not unique: Hevy and smaller trackers show them. Product pages therefore show DAUNTRA's authentic UI and explain the comparison workflow, without claiming category exclusivity. Pre-launch status is a real conversion disadvantage against downloadable competitors.
- Standalone 1RM, nutrition+training, strength-progress and PPL pages remain deferred because they would currently duplicate existing pages or lack distinct product proof/reviewed evidence. This is a deliberate limit on indexable surface, not a content-volume target.
- The two guide drafts and nearly empty guide hub were initially easy to over-count as SEO assets. They now stay `noindex` and absent from the sitemap until a source-linked packet and actual human approval exist. The 11-page sitemap contains only intentionally useful public routes, including legal/trust pages; the five acquisition URLs are the three product pages and two tools.

## Measurement and privacy challenge

- Local browser events were observed for `seo_landing_view`, `product_proof_viewed`, `tool_started` and `tool_completed` with only route, session first-touch, safe referrer/UTM and enum IDs. A mocked browser form run from `/tools/progressive-overload-calculator?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=compare_sets` sent a POST body with the correct landing path, UTMs and `first_touch_source=reddit`; the email appeared only in the body, not the URL. It produced `waitlist_form_started` and `waitlist_signup_success` after a mocked 201 response. The API request was intercepted before Supabase, so **no real lead was created**. The diagnostic build showed the slim PostHog client initializing and `capture()` being called, but no outbound collector request was observed from the synthetic browser; the SDK source contains a bot-event filter. The interceptor would have aborted such a request, so no production analytics data was sent. **End-to-end collector delivery remains unverified** and belongs in an approved staging/real-browser release test. New-lead attribution remains **not durable** while the optional production migration and server flag are unapplied. Browser success alone can represent a duplicate acknowledgement.
- The initial performance audit caught an eager root-level PostHog bootstrap missed in the first code read. It has been replaced by a lazy slim explicit-event layer with session replay/autocapture/external dependency loading/flags disabled and legacy event aliases retained. Cloudflare pageviews remain the baseline. This is a behavior/privacy change requiring review before deployment. A valid local Lighthouse run improved from 55 to 65 mobile performance, with 100 SEO/100 accessibility; a separate Inter variable-font experiment measured 63 and was reverted. Two later Lighthouse retries ended in local Chrome `NO_FCP` despite 200 HTML and successful Chromium screenshots, so **there is no valid final-run score**. Lab variance and a 4.5-second valid-run LCP mean performance is **not solved**.

## Creative and renderer challenge

- Reviewed the decoded canary contact sheet and key frames at phone aspect ratio. The final fallback uses authentic moving app UI from frame zero, five short beats across 15 seconds, a single early-access CTA, no unsupported UI or voice, and no required audio. The frame-zero bright-pixel proxy increased from ~0.11% in sampled Motion C outputs to 6.21%; first-to-one-second mean RGB difference rose from 4.14–5.08 to 8.82/255. These are **visual proxies**, not measured viewer retention.
- The HyperFrames canary failed locally even on an upstream blank starter; the successful MP4 was rendered by the isolated FFmpeg/Pillow fallback. Calling it a HyperFrames render would be false. HyperFrames has not demonstrated better output or reliability here, so no renderer adapter or publishing feature flag was added. Motion C and all existing factual/approval/idempotent publishing paths remain untouched. A controlled audience test would be needed to know whether Creative V2 produces more qualified signups.
